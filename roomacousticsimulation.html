Treatment priority order

1
Corner bass traps, all four floor-to-ceiling cornersBiggest low-frequency improvement. 20 cm Rockwool superchunk or floor-to-ceiling tube traps. Addresses the axial modes of the 2.77 m width around 62 Hz.
2
Side wall first reflection pointsControls early reflections and improves stereo imaging. 10 cm panels at ear-height mirror points.
3
Ceiling cloud above the deskEliminates ceiling flutter echo and comb filtering at the mix position. 10 cm panel, 5-10 cm air gap.
4
Front wall behind the speakersControls low-mid buildup. 10-15 cm broadband panel spanning the full width.
5
Back wall, lower half absorber plus upper diffuserBreaks up flutter echo and returns energy to the room without low-frequency buildup.
6
Rear corner soffit trapsTightens the low end further and addresses the 32 Hz fundamental of the length mode.

ADAM A7V specific tips

Boundary EQ. The A7V has built-in HF and LF shelving trims. With 95 cm or more to the front wall, set the low shelf to 0 or -2 dB. Closer than that, use -4 dB to tame bass buildup.

Tweeter dispersion. The ART tweeter has very wide horizontal dispersion, so first reflections off the side walls are very audible in a 2.77 m wide room. Side wall panels are not optional.

Port output. The A7V is front-ported, so it is less sensitive to rear wall distance than a rear-ported monitor. Keep 95 cm or more to the front wall anyway, for the axial mode reasons above.

SPL. 85-90 dB at the listening position. Avoid pushing past 95 dB reference in a room this small, because modal resonance gets much worse.

Narrow room caveat. The 2.77 m width produces an axial mode at 62 Hz. Every mix decision in the 50-80 Hz range is unreliable without corner bass trapping. This is priority one.
